# Hearthline 0.4.0: Coupled Factory Process Simulation

## Summary
Build one integrated release connecting Body Preparation, factory utilities, Forming, drying, glazing, firing, and final inspection. Material quality and equipment behavior must emerge from simulated physical conditions, not predetermined phase outcomes or generic quality scores.

The agreed target is **live 3D continuum physics, exact cross-platform replay, configurable faults, and adjustable time**, using an original basin and simple validation specimens. Both kilns remain, with recipe-dependent routing.

Your two-core, 6 GB laptop is the baseline environment. Full-factory 3D simulation may run substantially slower than real time. The release must demonstrate feasibility and numerical accuracy; optimization alone cannot guarantee either. “Full 3D” means discretized engineering models with documented constitutive assumptions, not molecular simulation or universally accurate predictions.

## Foundation And Numerical Architecture
- Add `hearthline-physics`, a `no_std` numerical crate beneath the engine. It owns spatial discretization and physical equations; the engine owns equipment behavior and control boundaries; simulation owns scheduling and allocated storage. Extend architecture and allocation guardrails accordingly.
- Replace phase-derived inventories, fixed transfer flows, and implicit infinite utilities with conservation-based evolution. Controllers request actions; equipment dynamics determine delivered flow, pressure, heat, movement, and completion.
- Use checked, nondimensionalized fixed-point numerical arithmetic with wide intermediates, specified rounding, and versioned mathematical functions. Overflow, division errors, and nonconvergence produce explicit simulation diagnostics, never silently saturated physical state.
- Implement conservative 3D finite-volume transport and flow, coupled to finite-element solid mechanics on compiled geometry. Use implicit time integration, stable mesh numbering, deterministic sparse iteration, and bounded nonlinear retries.
- Use spatially distributed continuum models for turbulence, dispersed gas, particles, and porous materials. Do not attempt to resolve every bubble, mineral particle, or turbulent eddy. Record these approximations and their validity ranges.
- Compile meshes, sparsity patterns, equipment instances, material properties, and connection boundaries into the immutable model. Share immutable geometry between instances, but never share mutable state between distinct batches or pieces.
- Preallocate field storage, solver workspaces, rollback buffers, and bounded transfer queues before sealing. Start with a configurable **1.5 GiB physics allocation budget**, including 25% reserve; measure total application memory against the laptop before accepting the canonical model.
- Run authoritative simulation work on one dedicated worker initially, keeping HTTP and rendering separate. Use sparse storage, reusable buffers, independently scheduled physical timescales, and incremental projections; never suspend physics merely because an area is off-screen.
- Introduce a single simulation clock. Provide pause, step, 1x, 2x, 5x, 10x, and headless execution. Requested speed affects pacing only; under load, show achieved speed without skipping physical steps or changing fidelity.
- Extend model contracts with `MaterialLot`, `ProductInstance`, `PhysicalPort`, `PhysicalConnection`, `PhysicsProfile`, and `FaultSchedule`. Keep appliance and connection YAML authoritative; reusable material and geometry documents are referenced by digest.
- Extend snapshots and replays to include spatial fields, material genealogy, solver state, random-generator state, controller timers, and queued transfers. Require identical normalized results on all four supported platform/architecture combinations, not merely identical recorded playback.

## Utilities And Production Physics
- **Compressed air:** add an enterable factory compressor station with central compressor redundancy, cooling, separation, receivers, dryers, filtration, drains, and distribution. Each process sector receives its own independent compressor/receiver package; high-pressure consumers additionally use explicitly connected boosters. A booster cannot manufacture supply when its inlet is unavailable. Storage, air quality, controls, and pressure losses follow the public [DOE compressed-air engineering references](https://www.energy.gov/cmei/ito/compressed-air-systems).
- **Air distribution:** model finite gas inventory, compression heat, pressure-dependent demand, regulator behavior, check valves, isolation, relief behavior, leakage, moisture transport, and condensate accumulation. Distinguish gauge/absolute pressure and actual/reference volumetric flow. Supply failures must affect actuator force, stroke time, process pressure, and available cycles.
- **Water and returns:** replace nominal route readings with spatial flow and transport through actual pipe volumes. Model pump characteristics, elevation, friction, valve position, receiving capacity, cavitation conditions, and start/stop transients. Use [EPA hydraulic reference cases](https://www.epa.gov/water-research/epanet) for compatible steady and extended-period benchmarks, not as a slip or compressed-air solver.
- **Treatment:** derive filtration, adsorption, softening, membrane recovery, settling, dewatering, sludge, and rejected streams from loading and equipment state. Track declared chemical species and charge balance where pH is calculated; do not average pH or invent a universal “purity” measurement. Keep body and glaze returns segregated.
- **Slip preparation:** model ingredient inventory and moisture, weighing and feeder delivery, dispersant dosing, mixing power, spatial concentration and temperature, particle-size populations, shear-dependent viscosity, thixotropy, screening, magnetic separation, aging, settling, and gas entrainment. Laboratory release requires simulated samples and measurements.
- **Material transport:** conserve material through pipe filling, residence time, mixing, flushing, isolation, leakage, and recovery. A crack releases material or admits surrounding fluid according to local pressure. Transport entrained air and contamination downstream rather than immediately changing every connected tank.
- **Glaze preparation:** apply the same inventory and transport foundation to milling, particle-size evolution, suspension stability, screening, storage, and application. Track coating distribution and thickness on each product, including overspray, recovery, and contamination.
- **Forming and drying:** couple actual delivered slip, mould permeability, pressure, drainage, and gas content to green-body formation. Solve spatial moisture, temperature, shrinkage, stress, and regularized damage within each product. Calibrate material-dependent laws against public experiments; published work shows why moisture-dependent properties matter to drying predictions. [Ceramic drying research](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jeurceramsoc.2021.07.042)
- **Firing and inspection:** add spatial thermal histories, airflow, radiation, bounded combustion chemistry, atmosphere, densification, shrinkage, thermal stress, and body/glaze interaction. Inspection measures observable defects with configurable resolution and uncertainty; it does not automatically know hidden solver state. Recipes govern acceptance, repair eligibility, second firing, rejection, and release to the existing logistics boundary.
- **Supporting systems:** account for electrical availability, motor loading, fuel supply, exhaust, cleaning, waste, maintenance isolation, and shared-service failures. Electrical and communication systems retain appropriate circuit/discrete models rather than becoming unrelated 3D electromagnetic simulations.

## Controls, Faults, And Operator Experience
- Preserve the four water HMIs, independent Slip/Glaze controls, equal mould authority, machine-PC supervision, and independent robot pendant. Add separate central air-generation/distribution operation and sector-local compressor controls.
- Replace remaining Rust-owned normal process sequences with executable controller sources using a documented bounded Structured Text subset. Add the typed arithmetic, timers, function blocks, PID behavior, task scheduling, and I/O diagnostics required by these processes.
- Keep the complete causal path: physical state → instrument → remote I/O/network → controller → output module → equipment. Separate command, electrical feedback, shaft movement, and useful output; a heartbeat alone must not establish pump or compressor health.
- Add sensor lag, noise, bias, drift, calibration, range limits, frozen values, communication loss, and sample age. Preserve local control during HMI or central-office outages where the modeled local dependencies remain available.
- Distinguish software pause from process Hold, Stop, and emergency actions. Removing actuator commands does not freeze pressure, inertia, stored heat, settling, or chemical evolution. Safety resets require healthy permissives; reset does not automatically restart equipment.
- Provide a separate engineering/instructor fault workspace for scheduled, immediate, gradual, and intermittent faults. Faults modify causes such as crack area, bearing condition, valve friction, or sensor bias; clearing an injection does not erase existing contamination, damage, or trapped energy.
- Extend existing draft transactions to materials, physics settings, geometry, recipes, and fault scenarios. Add revision-checked session clock/fault commands and read-only engineering field projections; regenerate Rust-derived API schemas and TypeScript contracts.
- Preserve physical and logical navigation. Add on-demand Three.js equipment/product inspection with field slices, mesh visibility, and legends. Operator HMIs show available measurements; engineering views may reveal underlying fields and numerical diagnostics.
- Add batch genealogy, sampling records, alarm acknowledgment, trends, maintenance history, recipe deployment, inspection dispositions, and requested/achieved simulation speed. Bound historian memory and stream archival data instead of retaining unlimited traces.

## Implementation Order And Acceptance
1. **Prove the numerical foundation first:** implement a coupled flow/receiver case and a drying specimen; demonstrate checked arithmetic, conservation, convergence, snapshot continuation, and exact four-platform replay. Compile a full-factory memory estimate before migrating the canonical model. Failure blocks expansion rather than silently reducing the agreed scope.
2. **Build utilities and transport:** deliver central/local/boosted air, water and return systems, physical connections, instrumentation, and fault propagation using reusable blueprints.
3. **Replace Body Preparation physics:** implement material balances, treatment, slip, glaze, laboratory workflows, and authoritative controller programs.
4. **Complete downstream coupling:** connect individual material lots to all four moulds, drying, coating, recipe-dependent kiln routes, and final inspection.
5. **Integrate and qualify the release:** finish operator workflows, spatial views, documentation, migrations, and full-process validation. These are internal milestones within one release, not separate promised feature releases.

- Verify analytical and manufactured solutions, mesh/time-step convergence, closed-system conservation, experimental comparisons, and sensitivity to uncertain material properties. Keep numerical verification distinct from physical validation, following the [NIST verification and validation framework](https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.IR.8298).
- Exercise central-air loss, exhausted local storage, booster inlet starvation, dryer failure, pump failure with a healthy heartbeat, sensor failure with healthy machinery, pressure-dependent slip leaks, contamination transport, drying cracks, firing defects, repair/refiring, and recovery.
- Require identical results for identical simulation-time commands across pacing settings, headless execution, restart, and all supported platforms. Test simultaneous faults, isolation boundaries, maintenance actions, and independent safety behavior.
- Preserve existing safety/network expectations and operator authority. Explicitly review changed timing and physics outcomes when replacing old fixtures; archive the previous model and replay evidence rather than silently overwriting their meaning.
- Retain constrained-stack tests, post-seal allocation checks, capacity reserves, bounded queues, and CI coverage gates. Add mesh/parser fuzzing, solver failure tests, memory profiling, browser interaction tests, and a complete production/residence-cycle soak.
- Keep `0.4.0` as the proposed release version. Maintain MIT licensing, vendor-neutral assets, public reference provenance, and current/previous schema readers. Unvalidated material predictions remain explicitly experimental; the release makes no commissioning, safety-certification, or real-plant defect-prediction claim.
